# round-table: design notes

## Why the seats are agents, not one voice

Each seat runs as its own read-only agent (`agents/<seat>.md`, generated by `tools/seats.py` from the company's `company.json`). Asking one model to "play the C-Suite" blends the voices and lets one seat answer outside its boundary. Separate agents each get only their own boundary in their instructions, so a CFO view on pricing is visibly out of bounds and says so.

## Why the Chief of Staff chairs and goes first and last

`company.json` gives the Chief of Staff intake, routing to the one owning Chief, and chairing the Round Table, with no domain decisions. Opening with the chair frames the topic and names the owning seat; closing with the chair turns the views into seed intents, each routed to one owner.

## Why at most six seats

`company.json` notes that a group chat holds at most six bots. The limit is kept as a boundary (EC-0002), not worked around: a bigger topic gets two tables.

## Status of seats

Only Phase 1 seats (Chief of Staff, CTO Chief, CTO Checker) are EXISTING. Every other seat is PROPOSED: it gives advice and its agent says so. A round table never activates a seat, grants authority, or resolves an OPEN item.

## Confidentiality

Minutes are recorded only when the founder asks, with `trail-player` (pmcro-core). A `roundtable` entry names exactly the seats that sat, and the writer keeps it in the gitignored `.trail-local/roundtable/`. It is a house rule, not a legal agreement.

## Not covered or not verified

- The agents were generated and checked for shape and read-only tools; a live conversation with them in Claude Code has not been recorded here.
- Phases are not in `company.json`; they come from the Company Foundation baseline and are fixed in `tools/seats.py`.
- Other hosts (Cursor, Codex, Copilot) may not load the `agents/` folder.
