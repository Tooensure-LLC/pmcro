# Affiliate disclosure

Status: company rules (ADR 0011). Not legal advice. Rules differ by country and platform and change; check the current rules and each affiliate program's terms before publishing. The US Federal Trade Commission's endorsement guidance, as generally understood, expects a clear and conspicuous disclosure of a material connection (such as earning commission) close to the recommendation, not buried in a footer or behind a link. VERIFY the current wording yourself; it was not fetched here.

## What the checker enforces

1. A disclosure sentence that mentions earning a commission or being an affiliate appears BEFORE the first affiliate link in the page.
2. Every affiliate link carries `rel` with `sponsored` (and `nofollow`).
3. No guaranteed-income or get-rich phrasing.
4. A privacy policy link exists.

## What the checker cannot enforce

Whether the disclosure is clear enough, whether claims are true, whether the program allows this kind of page, tax and legal duties, cookie or consent law. A human reviews these.

## Writing rules

- Say it plainly: "We earn a commission if you buy through links on this page, at no extra cost to you." Only say "no extra cost" if true.
- Describe what the product does and who it suits. Say what it does not do.
- No fake scarcity, fake reviews, or invented testimonials. No "you will earn" claims.
